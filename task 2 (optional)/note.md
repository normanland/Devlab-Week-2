# Regional Sales Breakdown and Genre Trends in Video Game Market

## Methodology

I used the `vgsales.csv` dataset from the Video Game Sales dataset on Kaggle. The original file contains 16,598 games and 11 columns covering game identity, release year, platform, genre, publisher, and regional sales.

Before starting the analysis, I checked the missing values and found 271 missing entries in `Year` and 58 in `Publisher`. Since the yearly trend depends directly on release year, I removed the rows where `Year` was missing. Missing publisher names were kept in the dataset and replaced with `"Unknown"` instead of removing those records. After cleaning, the working dataset contained 16,327 rows.

The analysis was completed in Python using pandas for aggregation and reshaping, and matplotlib/seaborn for visualization. I compared global sales across genres, platforms and publishers, calculated regional shares for North America, Europe and Japan, ranked the best-selling games, and reviewed how total market sales changed between 1980 and 2016.

Regional percentages were calculated against `Global_Sales`, so NA, EU and JP do not add up to 100% by themselves because the dataset also includes `Other_Sales`.

## Main results

Action was the largest genre in the cleaned dataset with about **1.72 billion units** in global sales, followed by Sports with **1.31 billion** and Shooter with **1.03 billion**.

Nintendo clearly led the publisher ranking with about **1.78 billion units** sold globally. Electronic Arts followed with roughly **1.09 billion**, while Activision ranked third at around **721 million**.

Among platforms, PS2 recorded the highest total global sales at approximately **1.23 billion units**. X360 followed with about **970 million**, while PS3 reached roughly **949 million**.

The yearly trend shows a long expansion through the 1990s and 2000s, with **2008** becoming the strongest year in the dataset at about **678.9 million units** sold globally.

At game level, **Wii Sports** is the clear outlier with **82.74 million units**, more than twice the sales of the second-ranked title, Super Mario Bros. at 40.24 million.

The decade comparison gives an interesting result. The **1980s had the highest average sales per game at about 1.84 million units**, even though the decade had only 205 titles in the cleaned dataset. By comparison, the 2000s generated by far the largest total volume, but with a much larger catalogue and a lower average of about 0.50 million units per game. For this reason, average performance and total market size should not be interpreted as the same thing.

## Four business insights

### 1. North America and Europe are similar in scale drivers, but Japan has a different genre structure

North America represents about **49.1%** of global sales in the cleaned dataset and Europe about **27.3%**, making them the two largest regional markets. Action leads both regions: it generated around **861.8 million units in NA** and **516.5 million in EU**. Sports and Shooter also appear near the top in both markets.

Japan behaves differently. Role-Playing is the leading genre there with about **350.3 million units**, accounting for roughly **27.3% of Japanese sales**. Action is a distant second at around 158.7 million. This suggests that a genre strategy that works well in North America or Europe should not automatically be transferred to Japan without adaptation.

### 2. Market leadership is concentrated, but the strongest names win for different reasons

Nintendo is the leading publisher with approximately **1.78 billion units** in global sales, well ahead of Electronic Arts at 1.09 billion. At platform level, PS2 leads with about **1.23 billion units**, followed by X360 and PS3.

The game ranking adds another layer to this concentration. Wii Sports alone sold **82.74 million units**, while the second-ranked title sold 40.24 million. In other words, market leadership is not only visible at publisher and platform level; individual blockbuster titles can also create unusually large gaps. This makes it useful to separate portfolio strength from the effect of a few exceptional releases.

### 3. The industry's strongest period was driven by scale, not necessarily by higher average performance per title

The yearly trend peaks in **2008 at 678.9 million units**, placing the late 2000s at the center of the industry's highest-volume period in this dataset. The 2000s also generated the largest decade-level total sales.

However, the average-sales-per-game comparison tells a different story. Games released in the 1980s averaged about **1.84 million units per title**, compared with roughly **0.50 million** in the 2000s. The 1980s figure comes from a much smaller catalogue, so it should not be read as evidence that the market itself was larger. Instead, the contrast shows how strongly catalogue size expanded over time: later decades produced much more total volume while spreading that demand across many more titles.

### 4. Regional preference gaps matter when evaluating genre opportunities

Looking only at global genre totals can hide important regional differences. Action is the overall leader because it performs strongly across the two largest markets, but Role-Playing has a much stronger relative position in Japan than in NA or EU.

This difference is visible in the regional comparison charts and in the preference-gap analysis. A global ranking therefore answers only part of the business question. For publishers planning launches, localization or marketing allocation, the more useful view is to combine overall genre size with each region's internal preference structure. A title can belong to a globally smaller category and still be highly relevant in a specific market.

## Notes and limitations

Sales values in this dataset are reported in millions of units. The analysis is descriptive and reflects the records available in the source dataset rather than the complete modern video game market.

Rows with missing release years were excluded because they could not be placed correctly in the yearly or decade analysis. Publisher names marked as `"Unknown"` were retained so that valid sales information was not lost unnecessarily.

The dataset also becomes less complete toward the end of the available period, so the decline after the late 2000s should be interpreted as a pattern in this dataset rather than a complete measure of all industry activity.
